The first 90 days in a new alliance role

New alliance leaders often start by launching something. I start by finding out what's already promised, what's stuck and what's worth more attention.

Days 1–30: Listen and map

  • Meet the executive sponsor and working lead at each priority partner, and ask what they'd change about working with us.
  • Meet our sales, product, marketing, finance and legal leaders to learn what they need from partners and what's frustrated them.
  • Map the portfolio: active, stalled and new relationships, open commitments, program tiers and unused benefits.
  • Agree on definitions of sourced and influenced revenue with sales operations and finance.

Output: a one-page current-state map and a short list of priority partners, shared with my leader.

Days 31–60: Focus and commit

  • Sign or refresh joint business plans with the priority partners.
  • Set up the meeting rhythm: working sessions, monthly pipeline reviews, quarterly executive QBRs.
  • Launch one co-sell play per priority partner, with a target account list.
  • Decide what to invest in, what to reset and what to pause, and explain why.

Output: signed plans, a running scoreboard and a clear invest, reset or pause decision for each partner.

Days 61–90: Prove and scale

  • Show early pipeline movement from partners, even if deals haven't closed yet.
  • Run the first executive QBR with real decisions on the agenda.
  • Put the health score and QBR prep report into the CRM so the rhythm doesn't depend on me.
  • Present a partner portfolio strategy for the next year to leadership.

Output: evidence the motion works, and a plan to grow it.

What I won't do in the first 90 days: launch a new program before I know why the current one isn't working.
