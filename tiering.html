Partner segmentation and tiering

Tiers are an internal tool. Partners don't see them. They decide where our time, funding and co-sell coverage go, and they change as partners' activity changes.

Segment first, then tier

  1. Partner type. Classify each partner by how it goes to market: systems integrator, consultancy, technology partner, reseller or managed service provider. This sets the co-sell approach and meeting rhythm.
  2. Capability. Map each partner to the solution areas it can deliver, with skill level and certification status for each.
  3. Commitment. Look at what the partner has actually put in: certified people, a signed plan, an executive sponsor, joint pipeline.
  4. Tier. Combine capability and commitment into an internal tier. The tier drives account assignment, funding and how much of our time the partner gets.

Moving between tiers

  • The alliance team reviews tiers twice a year against the entry criteria.
  • A partner can move up mid-cycle when it meets the criteria, so investment follows momentum.
  • When a partner slips, the alliance lead raises it through the joint plan and QBRs, focusing on the activity, not the tier.
  • Health score trends (see health scoring) inform the review, so a slipping partner gets attention before the formal review.

The most common mistake I see: tiers based on revenue alone. Revenue lags. A partner that is certifying people, building a plan and showing up to reviews will produce pipeline. A partner with past revenue and no current activity usually won't.
