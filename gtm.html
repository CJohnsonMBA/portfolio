Partner-led go-to-market plan

This is the plan I'd build for a data, AI or cloud vendor that wants partners to source and influence a real share of revenue, not just appear on a slide.

The problem

Most partner programs have a long roster and a short list of partners who actually sell. Funding gets spread evenly, enablement goes to whoever asks, and the field doesn't trust partner pipeline. The goal of this plan is to concentrate effort on the partners who can move the number, and to make their contribution visible.

Who to partner with

Four pillars

1. Focus

Segment and tier partners (see tiering) and pick a short list of priority partners per segment. Investment follows the tier, not the loudest request.

2. Enable

Enablement for both the partner's sellers and its technical teams: a short sales play, a demo they can run without us, and certification tied to what they'll actually deliver. I track whether partners use it, not just whether they attended.

3. Co-sell

Account mapping with each priority partner, a shared list of target accounts, and clear rules of engagement with our field. Every joint pursuit has an executive sponsor on both sides.

4. Co-market and fund

Market development funds go to campaigns with a pipeline target attached. I hold MDF to a pipeline return target (for example, five dollars of qualified pipeline per dollar of funding) and move money away from programs that miss it.

Partner economics

Partners commit to a vendor when it's good business for them. Before launching a motion, I check four things:

  • Services pull-through: how much billable work the partner earns around each deal.
  • Incentives: referral fees, resale margin or co-sell credit, and whether they reward the behavior we want.
  • Funding: MDF and funded proofs of concept that lower the partner's cost to try.
  • Effort to sell: how hard our product is to position, implement and support.

Twelve-month rollout

  1. Months 1–3: Focus. Audit the current portfolio, agree on definitions for sourced and influenced revenue, tier partners, and sign joint business plans with the first priority partners.
  2. Months 4–6: Activate. Launch enablement and co-sell plays, start account mapping, and fund the first campaigns against pipeline targets.
  3. Months 7–12: Scale. Run executive QBRs, move funding toward what's working, retire what isn't, and add the next wave of partners.
